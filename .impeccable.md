## Design Context

### Users
Studio potters, ceramic artists, and curious makers who want to move quickly from a drawn vessel profile to a trustworthy physical reference, a directly printable form, or reusable 3D-printed tooling for pouring plaster slip-casting molds. The tool should remain approachable for someone who understands clay and plaster better than CAD.

### Brand Personality
Quiet, tactile, and capable. Formwheel should feel like a well-kept studio tool: calm enough for exploration, precise enough to trust, and warm enough to belong beside clay, plaster, and metal equipment.

### Aesthetic Direction
A light, material-led studio interface with warm plaster neutrals, clay accents, dark ink, restrained metallic details, and editorial serif typography. Avoid generic CAD chrome, neon-on-dark technical styling, glossy AI gradients, and dense engineering panels. Controls should be compact without becoming fiddly, with touch targets that work on a tablet in a studio.

### Design Principles
1. Keep the form itself dominant; controls should orbit the work rather than obscure it.
2. Make scale and manufacturability visible in millimeters at every consequential step.
3. Prefer deliberate, reversible edits over interactions that make the viewport or object jump unexpectedly.
4. Model real ceramic-studio workflows and name parts the way makers use them.
5. Clearly separate trustworthy automatic geometry from previews, warnings, and steps that still require mold-maker judgment.
